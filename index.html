01 The Origin

Who Am I

Hello! I go by Kene. (Think penne with a K, so if you're suddenly hungry, blame my parents.)

The rest of this site gives you the breakdown of my professional history, but here is who I am at my core: someone who loves understanding people and solving meaningful problems. The most rewarding moments of my career have come from sitting down with customers and cross-functional teammates, listening to their frustrations, uncovering the actual root causes, and building solutions that make their lives noticeably easier. That curiosity and empathy are what ultimately led me into product management.

What drives me

  • User-first mentality: The best products are built by people who care deeply about the users they serve.
  • Continuous improvement: Every conversation is a chance to learn, every problem a chance to optimize.

When I'm offline

You'll usually find me following Chelsea FC, talking about cars, discovering new music, or enjoying the beauty nature has to offer. Whether I'm reverse-engineering how an engine works or failing spectacularly at a pottery wheel, I just love learning how things are put together, and how to make them work even better.

02 Data Analytics

NC Birth Records & Maternal Patterns

Research · R Studio, Excel
+ View Details

Public health datasets contain thousands of observations, making it hard to quickly identify maternal factors tied to infant health outcomes. I ran exploratory analysis and hypothesis testing in R on the North Carolina Birth Records dataset to evaluate maternal smoking, marital status, and age against birth weight.

Impact
  • Analyzed 1,000+ birth records using R and Excel
  • Identified maternal smoking as a statistically significant predictor of lower birth weight (p = 0.019)
  • Produced executive-ready visualizations translating findings into healthcare insights

04 Data Engineering

Provider Data Pipeline

Data Pipeline · Python, SQL, SQLite, REST API
+ View Details

Healthcare provider directories pulled from public registries carry stale entries, missing fields, and duplicates. I treated validation as the core deliverable: ingestion normalizes NPI API records into a relational schema, and 14 SQL rules score each one for validity, freshness, and duplication before export.

Impact
  • Loaded 601 unique Colorado providers from 6 API searches with zero data-integrity errors
  • Flagged 292 stale records and 18 missing credentials via automated SQL rules
  • Diagnosed and fixed a specialty-name mismatch causing a silent empty export
SQL validation output flagging stale and incomplete provider records.
